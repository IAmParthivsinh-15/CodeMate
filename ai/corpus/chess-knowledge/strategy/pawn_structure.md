# Pawn Structure

## Definition
Pawn structure is the arrangement of all the pawns on the board. Because pawns move slowly and can never move backwards, the pawn structure changes less than the position of the pieces, and it shapes the long-term plans of both sides. It decides which squares are strong or weak, which files are open, where each side should attack and which endgames are good. Strong players look at the pawn structure first when choosing a plan. Common features include doubled, isolated, backward and passed pawns, and the number of pawn islands.

## Weak pawn types
Several pawn structure features are usually weaknesses:
- Doubled pawns: two pawns of the same colour on one file, created by a capture. They cannot protect each other and the front one blocks the rear one, though they may control useful squares or open a file.
- Isolated pawn: a pawn with no friendly pawns on the neighbouring files. It must be defended by pieces, and the square in front of it can be occupied by an enemy piece.
- Backward pawn: a pawn that has fallen behind its neighbours and cannot safely advance because the square in front is controlled by an enemy pawn. It often sits on a half-open file where it can be attacked.
- Hanging pawns: two side-by-side pawns (often on c4 and d4) with no pawns on neighbouring files, strong when mobile but vulnerable when blockaded.

## Passed pawns
A passed pawn is a pawn with no enemy pawns in front of it on its own file or on the neighbouring files, so no enemy pawn can stop it from advancing. Passed pawns are a major strength in the pawn structure, especially in the endgame, because they force the opponent to use pieces to blockade them. A protected passed pawn is supported by another pawn; an outside passed pawn is far from the other pawns and can decoy the enemy king away. Passed pawns should usually be pushed, and a rook behind a passed pawn supports it best.

## Pawn islands
A pawn island is a group of pawns on adjacent files separated from other pawns by empty files. For example, White pawns on a2, b2, e4, f2, g2 and h2 form two islands. The fewer pawn islands you have, the easier your pawns are to defend, because pawns on neighbouring files can protect each other. As a rule of thumb, the side with fewer pawn islands has the better pawn structure. When you capture towards the center or avoid unnecessary splits, you keep your islands to a minimum.

## How to spot it in your games
To use pawn structure in your planning, ask before every pawn move and pawn capture:
- Will this create doubled, isolated or backward pawns, or an extra pawn island?
- Does it give my opponent a hole where a knight can settle?
- Do I have a passed pawn, or can I create one with a pawn majority?
Common mistakes are recapturing away from the center without reason, pushing pawns that leave squares permanently weak, and ignoring a passed pawn until it is too late to stop. Target your opponent's weak pawns with pieces, and keep your own structure compact.
