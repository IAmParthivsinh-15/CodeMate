# Isolated Queen's Pawn

## Definition
An isolated pawn is a pawn with no friendly pawns on either neighbouring file. The most important case is the isolated queen's pawn (IQP, also called the isolani): a White pawn on d4 with no White pawns on the c- or e-files, or a Black pawn on d5 with no Black c- or e-pawns. The IQP often arises from openings such as the Queen's Gambit (Tarrasch Defence), the Caro-Kann Panov Attack and some lines of the French and Sicilian (Alapin). It is a double-edged structure: dynamic in the middlegame, often weak in the endgame.

## Strengths of the isolated pawn
The side with the isolated pawn gains activity. The d4 pawn controls the key squares e5 and c5, which make excellent outposts for knights. The half-open c- and e-files give the rooks active roles, and pieces generally have more space. Typical plans for the IQP side:
- Place a knight on e5 and aim the bishops and queen at the enemy king, often with the queen and a bishop lined up on the b1-h7 diagonal.
- Lift a rook to the third rank (for example `Re1-e3-g3` or `h3`) to join a kingside attack.
- Prepare the pawn break `d4-d5` to open lines at the right moment, when your pieces are ready.
Avoiding exchanges helps the IQP side, because its strength is piece activity.

## How to play against an isolated pawn
To play against an isolated pawn, first stop it, then attack it. The square directly in front of the pawn (d5 against an IQP on d4) is the ideal blockade square: no pawn can ever attack it, so a knight placed there is very stable. Then:
- Exchange pieces, especially the attacking minor pieces, so the IQP side loses its activity.
- Pile up pressure on the pawn with rooks on the d-file and pieces aimed at it.
- Aim for an endgame, where the isolated pawn is a lasting weakness and the blockade square is a permanent outpost.
Take care to keep your king safe while doing this, because the IQP side will look for attacking chances.

## How to spot it in your games
When an isolated pawn appears, decide at once which role you have. If you own the IQP, ask: are my pieces active, is my knight on e5 (or e4 for Black), and is `d4-d5` possible soon? Avoid passive defence and unnecessary exchanges. If you face the IQP, ask: have I blockaded the square in front of it, and which trades bring me closer to a good endgame? A common mistake for the defender is allowing `d4-d5` at a moment when it opens lines against their king; a common mistake for the IQP side is trading pieces and drifting into a passive endgame.
