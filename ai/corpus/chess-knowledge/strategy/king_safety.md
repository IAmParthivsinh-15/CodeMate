# King Safety

## Definition
King safety is how well protected your king is from attack. Since losing the king ends the game, king safety is often more important than material. In the opening and middlegame, when many pieces are on the board, a safe king usually sits behind a shield of unmoved pawns in a corner, reached by castling. An unsafe king might be stuck in the center with open files, or have its pawn cover weakened so that enemy pieces can approach. In the endgame the picture changes: with fewer pieces left, the king becomes a strong fighting piece and should come to the center.

## How to keep your king safe
To keep your king safe:
- Castle early, usually within the first ten moves, especially if the center may open.
- Keep the pawns in front of the castled king (for example f2, g2, h2) where possible; every pawn move there creates weak squares.
- Keep at least one or two pieces near your king, such as a knight on f3 or f6, which is an excellent defender.
- Do not leave the back rank unguarded; a later `h3` or `...h6` can create an escape square (luft) at the right moment.
- Avoid opening lines towards your own king when your opponent has more pieces nearby.

## Signs that a king is in danger
Look for these warning signs of poor king safety:
- The king has not castled and the e-file or d-file is opening.
- The pawn shield has holes, for example after `g3` or `...g6`, where the bishop that guarded those squares has been exchanged.
- Enemy pieces, especially the queen and knights, are closer to the king than its defenders.
- Open or half-open files point at the king and enemy rooks can use them.
- Pawns are storming towards the king and will soon open lines.
When several signs appear together, defence should come first.

## How to spot it in your games
To judge king safety, count attackers and defenders near each king, and look at the pawns in front of it. Before pushing a pawn in front of your castled king, ask what squares it will stop guarding and whether your opponent can use them. Before grabbing a pawn with your queen, ask whether your king is safe enough to spend the time. If you were mated or lost to a sudden attack, check when you last had a chance to castle, or which pawn move in front of your king created the weakness that your opponent used.

## Key takeaways
King safety is about pawn cover, nearby defenders and closed lines. Castle early, do not weaken your pawn shield without reason, and keep pieces close enough to defend. Attack your opponent's king when it is uncastled or its pawn cover is broken. In the endgame, reverse the rule: activate your king and bring it towards the center, because with few pieces left the danger of mate is small.
