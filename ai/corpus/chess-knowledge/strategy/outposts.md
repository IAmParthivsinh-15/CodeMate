# Outposts

## Definition
An outpost is a square, usually in the opponent's half of the board, where your piece cannot be attacked by an enemy pawn and is supported by one of your own pawns. Because no enemy pawn can challenge it, a piece on an outpost is very stable and can only be removed by being exchanged for an enemy piece. Knights benefit most from outposts, since a knight needs a secure forward square to be effective, while bishops and rooks also use them. Outposts on the fifth or sixth rank (from your side) are the most powerful, especially in the center.

## Examples of outposts
Outposts appear in many common structures:
- In Sicilian lines where Black plays `...e5` with a pawn on d6, the d5 square becomes an outpost for a White knight, supported by the pawn on e4, because Black's c- and e-pawns can no longer attack it.
- Against an isolated queen's pawn on d4, the d5 square in front of it is an outpost for the defending side, and e5 is an outpost for the IQP side.
- When an opponent has played `...f6` and `...d6`, the e6 square can become an outpost if White can support it with a pawn on d5 or f5.
A knight on such a square often controls key squares in the enemy camp and restricts the opponent's pieces.

## How to create and use an outpost
To create an outpost, first find a square the enemy pawns can no longer attack, then support it with a pawn and route a piece there. Typical steps:
- Provoke or wait for enemy pawn moves that leave a hole.
- Exchange the enemy minor piece that could trade off your piece on the outpost, such as the bishop of the same colour.
- Manoeuvre a knight to the square, often via several moves (for example `Nb1-d2-f1-e3-d5`).
Once the piece is established, use it as a base to attack weaknesses and support other pieces.

## How to spot it in your games
When looking for a plan, scan the opponent's half of the board for squares no enemy pawn can attack. Ask: can my pawns support that square, and can a knight reach it in a few moves? Defensively, think carefully before moving a pawn that is the last guard of a central square, because the square in front of your pawns may become a permanent outpost for an enemy knight. If an enemy knight dominated your position in a game, find the pawn move that gave it the outpost and remember that move as the real mistake.
