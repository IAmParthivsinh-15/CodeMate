# French Defence

## Main idea
The French Defence begins `1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5`. Black prepares to challenge White's e4 pawn with `...d5` supported by the e6 pawn, producing a solid, counter-attacking setup. The typical French pawn structure has a Black pawn chain on e6 and d5, often facing a White chain on d4 and e5. Black accepts some lack of space and a light-squared bishop on c8 that is hemmed in by its own pawns, in exchange for a sturdy position and clear targets for counterplay, especially White's d4 pawn at the base of the chain.

## Main move orders
After `1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5`, White's main choices are:
- `3.Nc3`, defending e4. Black can play `3...Bb4`, the Winawer Variation, pinning the knight, or `3...Nf6`, the Classical Variation.
- `3.Nd2`, the Tarrasch Variation, which avoids the pin and keeps options of `c3`, for example `3...Nf6 4.e5 Nfd7`.
- `3.e5`, the Advance Variation, gaining space. The main line is `3...c5 4.c3 Nc6 5.Nf3 Qb6`, with Black piling pressure on d4.
- `3.exd5 exd5`, the Exchange Variation, which gives a symmetrical and fairly quiet position.

## Typical plans
In the French Defence, Black strikes at White's pawn chain with the pawn breaks `...c5`, aiming at d4, and `...f6`, aiming at e5. Black's queen often goes to b6 and knights to c6 and f5 or e7 to increase the pressure on d4. The light-squared bishop can be activated with `...b6` and `...Ba6`, or via d7 to b5, or exchanged. White uses extra space on the kingside, keeps the e5 pawn strong, and often attacks the Black king with pieces and pawn moves like `f4-f5`. In the Winawer, White often gets the bishop pair and doubled c-pawns after `...Bxc3+ bxc3`, while Black aims at those weakened pawns.

## Common traps and beginner mistakes
A classic trap arises in the Advance Variation: `1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.e5 c5 4.c3 Nc6 5.Nf3 Qb6 6.Bd3 cxd4 7.cxd4 Nxd4?? 8.Nxd4 Qxd4 9.Bb5+`. The bishop checks Black's king and at the same time uncovers the White queen on d1, which now attacks Black's queen on d4. After Black deals with the check, `10.Qxd4` wins the queen. (Once Black plays the careful `7...Bd7`, the trick no longer works because the bishop covers the b5-e8 diagonal; White may then offer the d4 pawn anyway with `8.O-O`, the Milner-Barry Gambit.) Other common mistakes: Black leaving the c8 bishop passive all game, and Black playing `...f6` too early, weakening e6 and the king.

## What to look for next time
When you play the French, remember the pawn chain rule: attack the base of your opponent's chain. As Black, aim `...c5` at d4 and `...f6` at e5, and find a plan for the c8 bishop. As White, protect d4 and keep your pieces ready for a kingside attack. Before capturing a central pawn in the Advance Variation, check whether a bishop move from d3 opens the d-file against your queen.
