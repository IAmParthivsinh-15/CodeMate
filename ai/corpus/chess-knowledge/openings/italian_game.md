# Italian Game

## Main idea
The Italian Game is one of the oldest openings, beginning `1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4`. White develops quickly and places the bishop on c4, where it aims at f7, the weakest point in Black's camp because only the king defends it. The opening follows classical principles: central pawns, fast development and early castling. It suits players of all levels, from beginners who want natural moves to top grandmasters who use its slow, manoeuvring versions. Black's two main replies are `3...Bc5` (the Giuoco Piano) and `3...Nf6` (the Two Knights Defence).

## Main move orders
The Giuoco Piano runs `1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.c3 Nf6`. Now White chooses between:
- `5.d4`, an immediate central strike that leads to open, tactical play.
- `5.d3`, the Giuoco Pianissimo ("very quiet game"), a slow setup where White castles, plays `Re1`, `Nbd2-f1-g3` and prepares `d4` later.
The Two Knights Defence runs `1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6`, counterattacking the e4 pawn. White often plays `4.d3`, a calm reply, or the sharp `4.Ng5`, attacking f7. After `4.Ng5 d5 5.exd5 Na5` Black gives a pawn for quick development and attacking chances.

## Typical plans
In the Italian Game White usually aims to build a center with `c3` and `d4`, castle kingside and use the f-file or a kingside attack if Black's king is weakened. In the quieter lines White manoeuvres the queen's knight to g3 and sometimes pushes `a4` to gain queenside space and give the bishop a retreat on a2. Black aims to challenge the center with `...d5` or `...d6` and `...exd4` at the right moment, keep the bishop on c5 or retreat it to b6 or a7, and castle quickly. Both sides watch the pins `Bg5` and `...Bg4` against the knights on f6 and f3.

## Common traps and beginner mistakes
The Fried Liver Attack is the best-known trap in the Italian Game. After `1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.Ng5 d5 5.exd5 Nxd5?!`, White can sacrifice with `6.Nxf7 Kxf7 7.Qf3+`, forcing Black's king into the open, where it faces a dangerous attack; the safer main line is `5...Na5`. Other common beginner mistakes in the Italian are:
- Black playing an early `...h6` or `...a6` and falling behind in development.
- White launching `Ng5` and `Qf3` or `Qh5` too early, which a well-prepared opponent refutes by developing with tempo.
- Both sides forgetting that f7 and f2 are only defended by the king until castling.

## What to look for next time
When you play the Italian Game, keep checking the f7 and f2 squares: count how many pieces attack and defend them after each move. As White, prepare `d4` with `c3` rather than rushing, and castle before attacking. As Black, answer `4.Ng5` in the Two Knights with `4...d5` and `5...Na5` rather than `5...Nxd5`, and in the Giuoco Piano make sure you can meet `d4` with a central counter such as `...exd4` or a well-timed `...d5`.
