# King's Indian Defence

## Main idea
The King's Indian Defence (KID) is a hypermodern reply to `1.d4`, starting `1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nc3 Bg7 4.e4 d6`. Black allows White to build a large pawn center with pawns on c4, d4 and e4, planning to strike at it later with pawn breaks. The bishop on g7, fianchettoed on the long diagonal, is a key piece, pointing at White's center and queenside. The King's Indian is known for sharp, double-edged games in which both sides attack on opposite wings. It suits players who like dynamic, fighting chess and do not mind some risk.

## Main move orders
The main line is the Classical Variation: `1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nc3 Bg7 4.e4 d6 5.Nf3 O-O 6.Be2 e5 7.O-O Nc6 8.d5 Ne7`, called the Mar del Plata Variation. White closes the center with `d5`, and both sides then play on the wings. Other important White systems are:
- The Sämisch, `5.f3`, supporting e4 and preparing `Be3`, `Qd2` and often queenside castling with a kingside pawn storm.
- The Four Pawns Attack, `5.f4`, grabbing maximum space in the center.
- The Fianchetto Variation, with `g3` and `Bg2`, a quieter positional approach.

## Typical plans
In the King's Indian Defence the typical plans follow the pawn structure. After White closes the center with `d5`, Black's plan is a kingside attack: the knight retreats from f6 (often to d7 or e8) to allow `...f5`, followed by `...f4`, `...g5` and `...g4` to open lines against the White king. White's plan is the queenside: pawn advances with `b4` and `c5` to open the c-file and attack d6 and c7. The game often becomes a race. If White keeps the center open instead, Black's pieces, especially the g7 bishop, pressure d4 and play `...exd4` or `...c5` at the right moment.

## Common traps and beginner mistakes
Common beginner mistakes in the King's Indian Defence include:
- Black playing passively without the key pawn break `...e5` or `...c5`, leaving White a free hand in the center.
- Black exchanging the g7 bishop, which leaves the dark squares around the king weak.
- White castling kingside and ignoring Black's `...f5` and `...f4` pawn storm, then being mated.
- Black trading queens early in a way that removes their attacking chances, for example after `dxe5 dxe5 Qxd8`, when care is needed to avoid a passive endgame.
- Black opening the f-file or center before completing development.

## What to look for next time
When you play the King's Indian, identify which wing each side should play on once the center is fixed, and count how many moves each attack needs. As Black, prepare `...f5` by moving the f6 knight, and keep the g7 bishop. As White, speed up the queenside expansion with `b4` and `c5`, and keep enough defenders near your king. In a race, every tempo matters more than a pawn.
