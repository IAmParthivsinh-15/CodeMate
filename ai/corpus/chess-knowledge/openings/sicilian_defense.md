# Sicilian Defence

## Main idea
The Sicilian Defence begins `1.e4 c5`. Instead of answering symmetrically with `...e5`, Black fights for the d4 square with a flank pawn. This creates an unbalanced position: if White opens the center with `d4`, Black usually trades the c-pawn for White's d-pawn, gaining a central pawn majority and a half-open c-file. The Sicilian is the most popular reply to `1.e4` at club and master level because it gives Black real winning chances, though it demands good tactical awareness and some theory.

## Main move orders
The main line is the Open Sicilian: `1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3`. Black then chooses a system:
- The Najdorf, `5...a6`, a flexible move that prepares `...e5` or `...e6` and queenside expansion with `...b5`.
- The Dragon, `5...g6`, fianchettoing the bishop to g7 to aim along the long diagonal at White's queenside.
Other common second moves for Black are `2...Nc6` and `2...e6`. White can avoid the Open Sicilian with the Alapin, `2.c3`, which prepares `d4` so that White can recapture with a pawn, or with quieter setups such as `2.Nc3` or `3.Bb5` lines.

## Typical plans
In the Open Sicilian White has a lead in development and more space, and often castles queenside and attacks on the kingside with pawn storms such as `f3`, `g4` and `h4`, or places pieces aggressively with `Bg5`, `f4` and `Bc4`. Black's plans usually involve pressure along the half-open c-file, queenside expansion with `...a6` and `...b5`, and central breaks like `...d5` or `...e5`. Black's extra central pawn often gives good endgames if Black survives the middlegame. Opposite-side castling makes many Sicilians races: whoever attacks faster usually wins.

## Common traps and beginner mistakes
A well-known trap from the Smith-Morra Gambit, a White gambit against the Sicilian, is the Siberian Trap: `1.e4 c5 2.d4 cxd4 3.c3 dxc3 4.Nxc3 Nc6 5.Nf3 e6 6.Bc4 Qc7 7.O-O Nf6 8.Qe2 Ng4 9.h3?? Nd4!`. The knight attacks White's queen, and if `10.Nxd4` then `10...Qh2#`, because `h3` removed the pawn from h2. Other typical Sicilian mistakes:
- Black playing `...e5` too early and leaving a permanent hole on d5 without enough compensation.
- White attacking without developing, so that Black's counterplay on the c-file arrives first.
- Black delaying castling so long that a central break like `e4-e5` or a sacrifice on e6 or d5 catches the king in the center.

## What to look for next time
When you play the Sicilian, decide early which side of the board is yours. As Black, keep the c-file in mind, watch for sacrifices on d5, e6 and b5, and be careful with pawn moves that weaken d5 or d6. As White, develop quickly, use your space and lead in development before Black's queenside play starts, and remember that with opposite-side castling, time is more important than material.
