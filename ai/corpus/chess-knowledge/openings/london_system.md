# London System

## Main idea
The London System is a setup for White built on `1.d4` and an early `Bf4`. Instead of memorising many lines, White aims for the same solid structure against almost any Black defence: pawns on c3, d4 and e3 (a "pyramid"), the dark-squared bishop developed outside the pawn chain on f4, knights on f3 and d2, the light-squared bishop on d3, and kingside castling. Because the plan hardly changes, the London is popular with club players who want a reliable opening with little theory, and it is also played by strong grandmasters.

## Main move order
A typical London System move order is `1.d4 d5 2.Bf4 Nf6 3.e3 e6 4.Nf3 c5 5.c3 Nc6 6.Nbd2 Bd6 7.Bg3`. White meets `...Bd6` with `Bg3`, keeping the dark-squared bishop and inviting Black to exchange it in a way that opens the h-file for White's rook. White continues with `Bd3`, `O-O` or sometimes `Qe2`, and later `Ne5`. The bishop must reach f4 before White plays `e3`, otherwise the pawn on e3 shuts it in; many players therefore play `2.Bf4` right away.

## Typical plans
White's plans in the London System include:
- Placing a knight on e5, supported by the dark-squared bishop and, once that bishop has dropped back to g3 or h2, sometimes by a pawn on f4, to build a kingside attack.
- Using the bishop on d3 and queen on c2 or f3 to aim at h7.
- Expanding in the center with `e4` once development is complete.
Black's most common plans are to strike in the center with `...c5`, attack the b2 pawn with `...Qb6` (the square b2 is left weak when the bishop leaves c1), and sometimes to exchange White's f4 bishop with `...Bd6` or `...Nh5`. A kingside fianchetto setup with `...g6` and `...Bg7` is another good way to play against it.

## Common traps and beginner mistakes
The London System's most frequent problem is the `...Qb6` attack on b2. After `1.d4 d5 2.Bf4 c5 3.e3 Qb6`, White must decide how to deal with the threat to b2, for example with `Qc1`, `Qb3` or a gambit with `Nc3`. Simply ignoring it loses a pawn and can let the queen harass the queenside. Other typical mistakes:
- Playing the London on autopilot without noticing Black's specific threats.
- Allowing Black to exchange the f4 bishop with `...Nh5` when that trade suits Black, without deciding in advance whether to retreat the bishop (for example to g5 or e5) or accept the exchange.
- Being too passive and never following up with `Ne5` or `e4`.

## What to look for next time
When you play the London System, keep your structure but check each Black move for a concrete threat. Watch the b2 pawn whenever Black's queen can reach b6, and check whether your f4 bishop can be attacked by a knight on h5 or a bishop on d6. Once your development is complete, look for an active plan with `Ne5` or `e4` rather than shuffling pieces.
