# Caro-Kann Defence

## Main idea
The Caro-Kann Defence begins `1.e4 c6` followed by `2...d5`. Like the French Defence, Black challenges White's e4 pawn with `...d5`, but it is supported by the c6 pawn instead of e6. The big advantage over the French is that Black's light-squared bishop is not blocked in: it can often develop to f5 or g4 before Black plays `...e6`. The Caro-Kann has a reputation for being solid and reliable, giving Black a healthy pawn structure and good endgames, at the cost of slightly slower development and less space.

## Main move orders
After `1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5`, White's main choices are:
- `3.Nc3` (or `3.Nd2`) `3...dxe4 4.Nxe4`. The Classical Variation continues `4...Bf5 5.Ng3 Bg6 6.h4 h6 7.Nf3 Nd7 8.h5 Bh7`, with White gaining kingside space and Black keeping a sound structure.
- `3.e5`, the Advance Variation, where Black usually plays `3...Bf5` first and then `...e6` and `...c5`.
- `3.exd5 cxd5`, the Exchange Variation, which can become the Panov Attack after `4.c4`, often leading to isolated queen's pawn positions.

## Typical plans
In the Caro-Kann Defence, Black develops the light-squared bishop outside the pawn chain, then plays `...e6`, `...Nd7`, `...Ngf6` and castles, often kingside but sometimes queenside. The pawn break `...c5` is a key way to challenge White's center later. White usually aims for space and quick development, using the lead to attack or to exploit the slight passivity of Black's position. In the Advance Variation, White may push kingside pawns like `g4` to harass the bishop on f5, while Black counters with `...c5` against the d4 pawn.

## Common traps and beginner mistakes
The most famous Caro-Kann trap is a smothered mate: `1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 dxe4 4.Nxe4 Nd7 5.Qe2 Ngf6?? 6.Nd6#`. The e7 pawn cannot capture the knight because it is pinned to the king by the queen on e2, and the king's other squares are blocked by its own pieces. Other common mistakes:
- Developing the bishop to f5 when it can be harassed by `Ng3` or `g4` and not having a retreat ready.
- Playing too passively and never breaking with `...c5`.
- In the Advance Variation, forgetting that the e5 pawn takes the f6 square from Black's knight.

## What to look for next time
When you play the Caro-Kann, get the light-squared bishop out before closing it in with `...e6`, and check where it will retreat if attacked. After `4.Nxe4 Nd7`, look out for the queen on e2 and the knight jump to d6. As White, use your extra space and look for ways to punish slow development, such as quick kingside pawn advances against the bishop on f5 or g6.
