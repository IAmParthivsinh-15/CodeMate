# Ruy Lopez

## Main idea
The Ruy Lopez, also called the Spanish Opening, begins `1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5`. The bishop attacks the knight on c6, which is the main defender of Black's e5 pawn. This does not threaten to win the pawn immediately, because after `Bxc6 dxc6 Nxe5` Black can regain it with `...Qd4` or `...Qg5`, but it creates long-term pressure on e5 and on Black's center. The Ruy Lopez is one of the richest openings in chess and a favourite of world champions. It rewards patience and understanding of pawn structures more than memorised traps.

## Main move orders
The main line is the Morphy Defence: `1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Ba4 Nf6 5.O-O Be7 6.Re1 b5 7.Bb3 d6 8.c3 O-O 9.h3`, the Closed Ruy Lopez. White prepares `d4` with `c3`, and `h3` stops a pin with `...Bg4`. Other important options:
- The Exchange Variation, `4.Bxc6 dxc6 5.O-O`, giving Black the bishop pair but doubled c-pawns.
- The Berlin Defence, `3...Nf6 4.O-O Nxe4 5.d4 Nd6 6.Bxc6 dxc6 7.dxe5 Nf5 8.Qxd8+ Kxd8`, a solid line that leads to an early queenless middlegame.
- The Open Variation, where Black captures on e4 with `5...Nxe4` in the Morphy line.

## Typical plans
In the Closed Ruy Lopez, White's plan is to build a strong center with `c3` and `d4`, reroute the queen's knight via `Nbd2-f1-g3`, and later attack on the kingside or open the queenside with `a4`. The bishop on b3 (and later c2) aims at Black's kingside. Black defends e5 firmly, gains queenside space with `...b5`, and seeks counterplay with `...c5`, `...Na5` to hit the bishop, or a central break with `...d5` when possible. Positions are often strategic and long, with pieces manoeuvring behind the pawns.

## Common traps and beginner mistakes
The Noah's Ark Trap catches White players who grab a pawn carelessly: `1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Ba4 d6 5.d4 b5 6.Bb3 Nxd4 7.Nxd4 exd4 8.Qxd4?? c5!`. The queen must move, and then `...c4` traps the bishop on b3, for example `9.Qd5 Be6 10.Qc6+ Bd7 11.Qd5 c4`. Other common mistakes are:
- White thinking `Bxc6` followed by `Nxe5` wins a pawn early, when Black regains it with a queen move.
- Black playing `...d6` and `...Bd7` passively without any plan for counterplay.
- Forgetting that after `...b5` the bishop needs a safe retreat, usually b3 then c2.

## What to look for next time
In the Ruy Lopez, keep an eye on the e5 pawn as White and on the bishop's retreat squares as Black's target. As White, prepare `d4` with `c3` and play `h3` before `d4` if `...Bg4` is annoying. As Black, remember that `...b5` and `...Na5` can trap or exchange the Spanish bishop, and check for tricks where capturing on d4 opens lines against a White queen that recaptures too early.
