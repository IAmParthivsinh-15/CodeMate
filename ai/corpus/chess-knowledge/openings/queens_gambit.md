# Queen's Gambit

## Main idea
The Queen's Gambit begins `1.d4 d5 2.c4`. White offers the c-pawn to lure Black's d5 pawn away from the center. It is not a real gambit in the usual sense, because if Black takes the pawn, White can usually regain it. The main idea is to gain central control: if Black's d-pawn leaves d5, White can build a big center with `e4`. The Queen's Gambit is one of the most respected openings in chess, leading to strategic positions where pawn structure and piece placement matter more than early tactics.

## Main move orders
After `1.d4 d5 2.c4`, Black has three main replies:
- The Queen's Gambit Declined (QGD), `2...e6`, supporting d5. A main line is `3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bg5 Be7 5.e3 O-O 6.Nf3`.
- The Queen's Gambit Accepted (QGA), `2...dxc4`. White usually regains the pawn calmly, for example `3.Nf3 Nf6 4.e3 e6 5.Bxc4`.
- The Slav Defence, `2...c6`, supporting d5 with the c-pawn and keeping the c8 bishop free to develop.
In the QGD Exchange Variation, White plays `cxd5 exd5`, giving a structure in which White often launches a minority attack with `b4-b5` on the queenside.

## Typical plans
In the Queen's Gambit, White aims for central control and queenside pressure: pressure on the c-file, a minority attack, or a central push with `e4` when well prepared. Black's problems usually revolve around the light-squared bishop on c8 in the QGD, and the plan is to free the position with `...c5` or `...e5` breaks. In the QGA, Black gives up the center temporarily to get free development and later strikes back with `...c5`. In all lines, Black should not try too hard to keep the extra pawn on c4.

## Common traps and beginner mistakes
The Elephant Trap punishes a greedy White pawn grab in the QGD: `1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bg5 Nbd7 5.cxd5 exd5 6.Nxd5?? Nxd5! 7.Bxd8 Bb4+ 8.Qd2 Bxd2+ 9.Kxd2 Kxd8`, and Black is a knight up for a pawn. White assumed the knight on f6 was pinned, but the pin was only relative. Other common mistakes:
- Black trying to hold the c4 pawn in the QGA with `...b5`, which often loosens the queenside after `a4`.
- Black leaving the c8 bishop undeveloped for the whole game.
- White pushing `e4` too early, before it is properly supported.

## What to look for next time
When you play the Queen's Gambit, as Black decide how you will free your position with `...c5` or `...e5` and how the c8 bishop will get out. Before assuming a piece is pinned, check whether the pin is absolute (against the king) or only relative. As White, prepare the central push with `e4` or queenside pressure, and in the QGA do not rush to regain the pawn at the cost of development.
