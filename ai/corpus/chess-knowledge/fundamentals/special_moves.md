# Special Moves

## Castling
Castling is a special move in which the king and one rook move at the same time. The king moves two squares towards the rook, and the rook jumps over to the square the king crossed. For White, kingside castling (`O-O`) puts the king on g1 and the rook on f1; queenside castling (`O-O-O`) puts the king on c1 and the rook on d1. For Black the squares are g8/f8 and c8/d8. Castling is legal only when all of these are true:
- Neither the king nor that rook has moved earlier in the game.
- All squares between the king and the rook are empty.
- The king is not currently in check.
- The king does not pass through or land on a square attacked by the opponent.
The rook itself may pass over an attacked square, so queenside castling is allowed even if b1 (or b8) is attacked.

## En passant
En passant ("in passing") is a special pawn capture. When a pawn advances two squares from its starting square and lands right beside an enemy pawn, that enemy pawn may capture it as if it had moved only one square. Example: a White pawn stands on e5 and Black plays `...d7-d5`. White may reply `exd6`, placing the pawn on d6 and removing the Black pawn from d5. The capturing pawn must be on its fifth rank (White) or fourth rank (Black). En passant must be played immediately on the next move; if you wait even one move, the right is lost. It only applies to pawns capturing pawns.

## Promotion
Promotion is the special move that happens when a pawn reaches the last rank (the eighth rank for White, the first rank for Black). The pawn must be exchanged immediately for a queen, rook, bishop or knight of the same colour, chosen by the player; it cannot stay a pawn or become a king. You may promote even if the original piece is still on the board, so having two or more queens is legal. Choosing anything other than a queen is called underpromotion. It is occasionally correct: promoting to a knight can give check or fork pieces, and promoting to a rook or bishop can avoid stalemating an opponent who has no other moves.

## Common mistakes with special moves
Beginners lose games to the special moves in predictable ways:
- Trying to castle out of check, or through a square attacked by an enemy bishop or knight. It is illegal, and in over-the-board play the touched king may then have to move.
- Moving the king once "just to test" and losing the right to castle forever.
- Forgetting that en passant exists, so a pawn that seems safe beside an enemy pawn is actually capturable.
- Promoting automatically to a queen when that leaves the opponent with no legal move, which is stalemate and only a draw.

## What to look for next time
Before castling, check the squares the king will cross and land on: is any enemy piece aiming at them? When an opponent's pawn jumps two squares beside your pawn on your fifth rank, ask whether capturing en passant helps you, because the chance disappears after one move. When your pawn is about to promote, count your opponent's legal moves after the new piece appears; if they have none and are not in check, pick a different piece or a different move to avoid stalemate.
