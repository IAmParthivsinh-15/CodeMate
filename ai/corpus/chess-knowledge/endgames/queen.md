# Queen Endgames

## King and queen against king
Checkmating with king and queen against a lone king is a basic queen endgame technique that every player should know. The queen alone cannot mate; the king must help. The method is:
- Use the queen to restrict the enemy king to a shrinking box, often by placing the queen a knight's move away from it, which keeps it boxed in without checking.
- Push the enemy king to the edge of the board.
- Bring your own king close, usually to a square two squares away from the enemy king, facing it.
- Deliver mate with the queen supported by your king, for example Black king h8, White king f6 and `Qg7#`.
The mate can usually be forced in about ten moves from most positions.

## Avoiding stalemate
The biggest danger in the king and queen against king queen endgame is stalemate. When the enemy king is in the corner, a queen that takes away all its squares without giving check leaves it with no legal move, which is a draw. For example, with the Black king on h8 and a White queen on f7 or g6 (both a knight's move from h8), Black has no legal moves and is not in check: stalemate. Once the enemy king is on the edge, stop making queen moves that restrict it further and bring your king in instead, and before every move check that the opponent still has a legal move.

## Queen against pawn on the seventh
A queen against a pawn on the seventh rank supported by its king is a classic queen endgame. Against a center pawn or knight pawn (b, d, e or g), the queen wins: it uses checks and pins to force the defending king to step in front of its own pawn, which gains a tempo for the attacking king to approach. Repeating this manoeuvre brings the king close enough to win the pawn or mate. Against a rook pawn (a or h) or a bishop pawn (c or f), the defender often draws if the attacking king is far away, because of stalemate tricks: the defending king goes into the corner, and capturing the pawn or forcing it to block would leave no legal moves.

## Queen endgames with pawns
Queen endgames with several pawns are dominated by checks and king safety. The side with the safer king often wins, even without extra material, because the queen can deliver long series of checks and pick up loose pawns. Passed pawns, especially far-advanced ones, are extremely strong, because the queen can escort them while blocking checks. The defender's main resource is perpetual check, so the attacker must plan how the king will hide from checks before pushing pawns.

## What to look for next time
In queen endgames, practise the king and queen mate until you can do it quickly and without stalemate: box the king in, bring your own king, then mate. When your queen restricts a cornered king, count its legal moves before each move. Against a pawn on the seventh, check its file: center and knight pawns lose to the queen, while rook and bishop pawns may draw if your king is too far away. In queen endgames with pawns, look for perpetual check chances whenever you are worse.
