# Endgame Principles

## Definition
Endgame principles are general guidelines for the final phase of the game, when most pieces have been exchanged. The endgame has different priorities from the middlegame: the danger of checkmate is much smaller, so the king becomes an active piece, and pawns become more valuable because they can promote. Following a few endgame principles helps you win won positions, save difficult ones, and avoid wasting a hard-earned advantage. Many of them are the opposite of middlegame habits, which is why players often go wrong at this stage.

## Activate the king
The first of the endgame principles is to activate your king. With queens and most pieces gone, the king is a strong fighting piece that can attack and defend like any other. Bring it towards the center, or towards the side of the board where the pawns are, as soon as it is safe. An active king supports its own passed pawns, blocks enemy ones, and attacks weak pawns. In many endgames, the side whose king arrives first wins. Leaving the king on its castled square in an endgame is one of the most common mistakes club players make.

## Passed pawns must be pushed
Passed pawns are the main winning weapon in the endgame, and the principle "passed pawns must be pushed" reminds you to advance them, supported by your king and pieces. The further a passed pawn advances, the more it ties down the opponent's pieces. Other pawn-related endgame principles:
- Create a passed pawn with a pawn majority on one side of the board.
- An outside passed pawn, far from the other pawns, is especially strong because it decoys the enemy king.
- Blockade enemy passed pawns, ideally with a piece directly in front of them.
- Rooks belong behind passed pawns, both yours and the opponent's.

## Converting an advantage
When you are ahead in material in the endgame, useful principles are:
- Trade pieces, not pawns: fewer pieces make your extra material more decisive, while each pawn exchange brings the opponent closer to a draw.
- Do not rush: improve your position step by step before starting decisive action.
- Apply the principle of two weaknesses: attack one weakness, then create a second one on the other side of the board, so the defender cannot cover both.
- Watch out for stalemate and fortress positions in which extra material is not enough.
When defending, do the opposite: trade pawns, look for drawing structures, and keep your pieces active.

## What to look for next time
When you reach an endgame, ask: is my king active, and where are the passed pawns? Can I create a passed pawn? Which trades help me? If you are ahead, count whether the resulting pawn ending or simplified position is really winning before you trade. If you are behind, search for known drawing ideas such as opposite-coloured bishops, the wrong-coloured rook pawn or the Philidor defence. After a game, check whether your king joined the fight early enough; this single principle wins many endgames.
