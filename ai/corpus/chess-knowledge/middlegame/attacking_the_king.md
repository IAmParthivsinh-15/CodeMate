# Attacking the King

## Definition
Attacking the king means bringing enough force against the enemy king to checkmate it or win large amounts of material as the opponent defends. A successful king attack needs more attackers than defenders near the king, open lines (files or diagonals) towards it, and usually some weakness in the pawn cover. Attacks are most promising when the enemy king is still in the center, when its pawn shield has been weakened, or when kings have castled on opposite sides, allowing pawn storms without endangering your own king.

## When to attack the king
Before attacking the king, check that the conditions are right:
- You have more pieces near the enemy king than your opponent has defending it.
- The center is closed or under your control, so your opponent cannot counterattack there.
- There is a target: an uncastled king, weakened pawns such as `...g6` or `...h6`, or an open file towards the king.
- Your own king is safe enough to allow you to commit pieces.
Attacking without these conditions often just weakens your own position.

## Attacking methods
Common methods for attacking the king include:
- Bringing more pieces: rook lifts along the third rank, knights to f5, g5 or h5, and the queen joining last.
- Pawn storms: pushing the g- and h-pawns to open files, especially when kings are castled on opposite sides.
- Opening lines with sacrifices, such as the Greek gift sacrifice `Bxh7+` against a castled king, which works when a knight can follow with `Ng5+` and the queen can join via h5.
- Removing key defenders, especially a knight on f6, by exchange or sacrifice.
- Using checkmate patterns like the back-rank mate or mates on g7 and h7 with queen and bishop.

## How to defend against an attack
To defend against an attack on your king, bring defenders closer, exchange the opponent's most dangerous attacking pieces, and avoid unnecessary pawn moves in front of your king. Counterattack in the center, because an attack on the wing is best met by action in the center. Sometimes returning material to break the attack is the best practical choice. Look for forcing moves of your own, since even a strong attack can be stopped by a well-timed check.

## How to spot it in your games
In your games, look at the area around each king after every few moves: count attackers and defenders and note open lines. If you were mated, find the move when your opponent's attack became unstoppable and ask what defender was missing or which pawn move let it in. When attacking, keep bringing new pieces rather than attacking with one or two. A common mistake is starting an attack too early, before the pieces are ready, which gives the defender time to consolidate.
