# Calculation

## Definition
Calculation is the skill of working out concrete move sequences in your head: "if I play this, they play that, then I play this". It is how you check tactics, verify that a piece is safe, and choose between moves when the position is sharp. Good calculation is not about seeing twenty moves ahead; it is about looking at the right moves, seeing the positions accurately, and stopping to evaluate at the right moment. Most games at club level are decided by calculation errors, such as missing an opponent's capture or check, rather than by strategy.

## Candidate moves
The first step in calculation is choosing candidate moves: the handful of moves worth examining in the current position. Instead of analysing the first move you see, list two to four reasonable options, then calculate each one. This prevents "tunnel vision", where a player falls in love with one idea and misses a better one. Good candidate moves often include forcing moves, moves that improve your worst piece, moves that stop the opponent's plan, and the move your intuition suggests. Compare the positions at the end of each line and choose the best.

## Checks, captures and threats
When looking for candidates in calculation, always start with forcing moves: checks, captures and threats, in that order. They limit your opponent's replies, which makes the line easier to calculate and more likely to work tactically. Do this for both sides:
- What checks, captures and threats do I have?
- What checks, captures and threats does my opponent have after my move?
Many tactics, like forks, pins and mating combinations, begin with a check or capture that looks surprising. Scanning forcing moves every move is the single best habit for finding tactics and avoiding them.

## The blunder check
The blunder check is the final step in calculation before you move. After deciding on a move, pause and look at the position as if the move were already played. Ask:
- Which of my pieces are now undefended or attacked?
- What are my opponent's forcing replies (checks, captures, threats)?
- Did my move stop defending something important, like the back rank or a loose piece?
If any answer is dangerous, reconsider. The blunder check takes only a few seconds, but it prevents most one-move blunders.

## How to spot it in your games
When you review a game where you miscalculated, find the exact move you overlooked. Was it an opponent's check or capture? A quiet move you did not consider? A position you evaluated wrongly at the end of the line? Common calculation mistakes include stopping one move too early, forgetting that the opponent can play a zwischenzug, and imagining pieces on squares they have left. To improve, solve tactical puzzles without moving the pieces, and in your games always write down or say your candidate moves before choosing.
