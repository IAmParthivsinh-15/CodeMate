# Deflection

## Definition
Deflection is a tactic that forces an enemy piece away from an important job, usually defending a square, a piece or the king. You attack or offer something that the defender cannot ignore, and when it moves or captures, the thing it was guarding becomes vulnerable. Deflection often involves a sacrifice or a forcing exchange. It is closely related to the overloaded piece, where one defender has too many duties, and to removing the defender, where the guard is captured outright. The key difference is that in a deflection the defender is still on the board but has been pulled to the wrong square.

## Example of deflection
Consider this position. White: king g1, queen a5, rook e1, pawns f2, g2, h2. Black: king g8, queen c7, rook c8, pawns f7, g7, h7. Black's rook on c8 is the only piece guarding the back rank, so `Re8+` right away would be met by `...Rxe8`. White instead plays `1.Qxc7!`. If Black recaptures with `1...Rxc7`, the rook has been deflected from the eighth rank, and `2.Re8#` is checkmate because the pawns on f7, g7 and h7 block the king's escape. If Black does not recapture, White is simply a queen up.

## How to use deflection
To use deflection, first find the defender that holds your opponent's position together. Ask what it guards and what would happen if it were gone. Then look for a forcing move that makes it leave:
- A capture it must recapture, as in the example above.
- A check that it must block or capture.
- An attack on a more valuable piece that it has to deal with.
- A sacrifice on a square it controls.
Deflection works best when the follow-up is immediate and decisive, such as a checkmate or a large material gain.

## How to spot it in your games
When you look at your own position, notice pieces that have one critical defensive job, such as a rook guarding the back rank or a queen guarding a mating square. Those pieces can be deflected. Next time, before capturing an offered piece, ask: what was my piece defending before this capture, and is that still safe after it moves? Giving your king an escape square (such as `h3` or `...h6` to create luft) removes many back-rank deflection tricks. For your own play, when a direct threat is defended exactly once, look for a way to make that single defender move.
