# Removing the Defender

## Definition
Removing the defender is a tactic in which you capture, exchange or drive away the piece that protects an important square or piece. Once the guard is gone, the square or piece it was protecting can be attacked successfully. The defender might be protecting a piece you want to win, a square where you want to checkmate, or a pawn that holds the position together. The idea is closely related to deflection and overload, but here the defender is usually eliminated outright, often by capturing it even at the cost of material.

## Example of removing the defender
A classic example targets the h7 square in front of a castled king. White: king g1, queen h3, bishops on d3 and g5. Black: king g8, rook f8, queen d8, knight f6, pawns f7, g7, h7. White's queen and bishop on d3 both aim at h7, and the only piece defending h7 is the knight on f6. White plays `1.Bxf6!`, removing the defender. If Black recaptures with `1...gxf6` or `1...Qxf6`, then `2.Qxh7#` is checkmate, because the queen on h7 is protected by the bishop on d3 and the king has no escape square.

## Ways to remove a defender
To remove a defender you can:
- Capture it, even if you give up material, when what you win afterwards is worth more.
- Attack it with a pawn or smaller piece so it must move away.
- Exchange it off, for example trading your bishop for the knight that guards a key square.
- Undermine it by attacking the pawn that supports it, so it becomes loose.
- Block its line of defence by placing a piece between it and the square it guards (interference).
Always check that your follow-up works in every variation after the defender disappears.

## How to spot it in your games
When an attack seems almost to work, find the one piece that stops it. That is the defender to remove. Ask: can I capture it, chase it or exchange it? Defensively, notice when a single piece of yours is holding a vital square, such as a knight on f6 guarding h7 or a bishop guarding a back-rank square. If that piece can be captured or attacked with tempo, add a second defender or reduce the danger before your opponent strikes. When you review a lost game, identify the moment your only defender was taken and ask whether you could have supported it earlier.

## Key takeaways
Removing the defender turns a defended target into an undefended one. First find the target, then the single piece guarding it, then a forcing way to eliminate that guard. Material sacrifices are often justified if the follow-up wins more material or delivers mate. In your own position, avoid relying on one piece to guard a critical square, and add a second defender when your opponent starts aiming several pieces at it.
