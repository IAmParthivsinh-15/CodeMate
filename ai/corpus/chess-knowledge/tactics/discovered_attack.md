# Discovered Attack

## Definition
A discovered attack happens when one piece moves out of the way and uncovers an attack by another piece standing behind it on the same line. The piece behind must be a long-range piece: a bishop, rook or queen. The move is powerful because two things happen at once: the moving piece can make its own threat, while the uncovered piece attacks something else. If the uncovered attack is on the king, it is called a discovered check. If both the moving piece and the uncovered piece give check at the same time, it is a double check, and the only legal reply is to move the king, since you cannot block or capture two checking pieces with one move.

## Example of a discovered attack
A classic discovered check appears in the Petroff Defence. After `1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6 3.Nxe5 Nxe4?! 4.Qe2 Nf6??`, White plays `5.Nc6+`. The knight moving from e5 uncovers the queen on e2, which now gives check along the open e-file to the king on e8. At the same time the knight on c6 attacks the Black queen on d8. Black must deal with the check, for example with `5...Be7`, and then `6.Nxd8` wins the queen. Capturing the knight on c6 does not help, because it does not stop the check from the queen.

## How to set up a discovered attack
To create a discovered attack, you need a battery-like arrangement: your long-range piece aimed at a target, with one of your own pieces in between. Then look for a move by the front piece that creates a second threat. The best candidates are:
- A check or capture by the front piece, so the opponent has no time to answer the uncovered attack.
- A knight jump, since knights create threats in unexpected directions.
- An attack on an undefended piece or a mate threat.
Double checks are often decisive because the opponent's king must move, even if every other defensive resource is available.

## How to defend against it
To defend against a discovered attack, notice when an enemy long-range piece is aimed at your king or queen through one of the opponent's own pieces. That front piece is a loaded gun: any move it makes can unleash the attack. Options include moving your king or queen off the line in advance, putting a defended piece in between, or removing the front piece before it moves. Also avoid leaving pieces undefended while such a line exists, since the moving piece will aim at them.

## How to spot it in your games
When reviewing a game where you lost material to a discovered attack, find the line (file, rank or diagonal) on which the uncovering piece was aiming. Next time, check every enemy rook, bishop and queen and trace its line through the pieces in front of it, including your opponent's own pieces. Ask: if that front piece moves with a threat, can I handle both threats at once? For your own attacks, look for moments when one of your pieces is blocking your queen, rook or bishop from a target, and ask what that blocking piece could attack if it moved.
