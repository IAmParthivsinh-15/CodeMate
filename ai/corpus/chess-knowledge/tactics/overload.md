# Overloaded Piece

## Definition
An overloaded piece is a defender that has two or more jobs at the same time and cannot actually do all of them. For example, a rook might be guarding both the back rank and a piece further up the board. As long as nobody tests it, the position looks fine. But if the opponent attacks one of the things it guards, the defender must choose: if it deals with the first job, the second one fails. Exploiting overload is often done by capturing on one of the defended points so that the defender must recapture, abandoning its other duty.

## Example of overload
Consider this position. White: king g1, rook e1, knight f3, pawns f2, g2, h2. Black: king g8, rook d8, knight d4, pawns f7, g7, h7. Black's rook on d8 has two jobs: it defends the knight on d4 and it guards the back rank against `Re8+`. White plays `1.Nxd4!`. If Black recaptures with `1...Rxd4`, the rook has left the eighth rank and `2.Re8#` is checkmate. If Black does not recapture, White has simply won a knight. The rook was overloaded.

## How to exploit an overloaded piece
To exploit overload, list your opponent's defenders and write down what each one protects. If one piece appears twice, it may be overloaded. Then:
- Capture or attack one of the things it protects, ideally with a forcing move.
- Make sure the second duty is a real threat, such as mate or a large material gain, once the defender leaves.
- Consider the order of moves: sometimes you should first remove or exchange a second defender so that the overloaded piece stands alone.
Overload often combines with deflection, because the forcing capture deflects the overworked defender.

## How to spot it in your games
To avoid overload in your own position, count what each of your defenders is doing. Warning signs include one rook guarding both the back rank and a loose piece, a queen defending two pieces far apart, or a single pawn protecting two squares that matter. Next time, when you notice that a defender has two jobs, add a second defender or reduce the duties, for example by moving the loose piece to safety or giving your king an escape square. When you lose material and cannot see why, check whether one of your pieces was asked to defend two things at once.
