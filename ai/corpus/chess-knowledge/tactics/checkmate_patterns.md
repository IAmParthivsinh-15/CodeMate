# Checkmate Patterns

## Back-rank mate
The back-rank mate is one of the most common checkmate patterns. It happens when a king sits on its first rank behind its own unmoved pawns, and an enemy rook or queen checks along that rank. Example: Black king on g8 with pawns on f7, g7 and h7, and a White rook on e1 with an open e-file. `Re8#` is checkmate: the pawns block every escape square and nothing can capture the rook or interpose on f8. To defend, keep a rook or queen guarding the back rank, or make an escape square ("luft") with a pawn move such as `h3` (for White) or `...h6` (for Black) when you have time.

## Smothered mate
Smothered mate is a checkmate pattern delivered by a knight against a king whose own pieces surround it so it cannot move. The classic case: Black king on h8, Black rook on g8, pawns on g7 and h7, and a White knight lands on f7 with check. The king has no square and the knight cannot be blocked, so it is mate. It is often prepared by a queen sacrifice on g8 that forces the rook to capture and fill the last free square, followed by the knight check. A related opening example is the Caro-Kann trap `1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 dxe4 4.Nxe4 Nd7 5.Qe2 Ngf6?? 6.Nd6#`, where the e7 pawn is pinned by the queen and cannot capture the knight.

## Scholar's mate and early attacks on f7
Scholar's mate is the most famous beginner checkmate pattern. The queen and bishop team up against f7 (or f2), the weak square defended only by the king at the start. Example: `1.e4 e5 2.Bc4 Nc6 3.Qh5 Nf6?? 4.Qxf7#`. The queen on f7 is protected by the bishop on c4, and the king is boxed in by its own pieces. Black could defend with `3...g6` or `3...Qe7`. Even faster is Fool's mate, `1.f3 e5 2.g4 Qh4#`, which shows the danger of weakening the diagonal to your king in the first moves.

## Ladder mate and other patterns
The ladder mate (also called the lawnmower or rolling mate) uses two rooks, or a rook and queen, to push a king to the edge of the board. One rook cuts off a rank while the other checks on the next rank, and they leapfrog towards the edge until the king has nowhere to go. A few more named patterns:
- Arabian mate: a rook and knight work together in the corner, for example a White rook on h7 protected by a knight on f6 mating a king on h8.
- Anastasia's mate: a knight and rook trap a king on the edge beside its own pawn.
Knowing these checkmate patterns by sight makes it much easier to spot mates in your own games.

## How to spot it in your games
To find mates and avoid being mated, look at the squares around each king. Ask:
- Which escape squares does the king have, and which are blocked by its own pieces?
- Is the back rank protected, or is a single rook check enough?
- Is f7 (or f2) attacked twice and defended only by the king?
- Can a knight check on a square where the king is surrounded by its own pieces?
If you were mated on the back rank, next time create luft with a timely pawn move. If you fell for Scholar's mate, remember to defend f7 and develop pieces rather than moving pawns around the king.
