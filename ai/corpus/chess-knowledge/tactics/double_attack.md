# Double Attack

## Definition
A double attack is any move that creates two separate threats at once. The opponent can usually answer only one of them, so the other threat succeeds. A fork, where one piece attacks two targets, is the most common kind of double attack, but the idea is broader. The two threats can come from two different pieces, for example a discovered attack. One threat can be an attack on a piece and the other a threat of checkmate. Or one threat can be to capture and the other to promote a pawn. The queen is the strongest double-attacker because it moves in eight directions.

## Example of a double attack
A simple double attack appears early in many beginner games. After `1.e4 e5 2.Qh5`, the White queen attacks two things at once: the pawn on e5 and the pawn on f7 next to Black's king. Black must defend carefully. `2...Nc6` protects e5, and f7 is protected by the king for now. After `3.Bc4` White threatens `Qxf7#`, so Black needs another defensive move such as `3...g6`, which blocks the queen's diagonal and attacks it. If White continues `4.Qf3`, the threat on f7 is renewed, and `4...Nf6` covers it. Each time, Black must see both threats created by one move.

## How to create a double attack
To create a double attack, look for targets first. Good targets are undefended pieces, the enemy king, weak pawns and squares where you could checkmate. Then look for one move that hits two targets. Useful patterns:
- A queen move that checks the king and attacks a loose piece at the same time.
- A move that threatens mate and also attacks a piece.
- A pawn push that attacks two pieces standing side by side.
- A capture that also creates a new threat, so the opponent has no time to recapture.

## How to spot it in your games
To avoid double attacks, keep your pieces defended and do not leave two targets that one enemy piece can hit together. Before each move ask: after my move, which of my pieces are loose, and can one enemy move attack two of them, or attack one while threatening my king? When you lose material to a double attack, the usual cause is a loose piece far from the rest of your army, or a weak back rank that let your opponent combine a mate threat with an attack on something else.
